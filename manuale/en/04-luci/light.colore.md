# Light color and temperature (Kelvin)

**Where:** select the light (**Selection** tab, or **Scene → Lights**) → **Color** (any color) and **Temperature** (buttons 2700 K, 4000 K,
5500 K, 6500 K, 9000 K). In Simple mode, **Light color**: Warm 3200 K, Neutral 5500 K, Cool
7500 K, applied to all lights at once.

**What it does:** the color temperature is the color of the light as photographers describe it:
low values are warm (yellow-orange, like a bulb or a candle), around 5500 K is the white of
daylight and studio flashes, high values are cool (bluish, like an overcast sky or shade).

![2700 K: warm, like a household bulb](img/comune/light_temperature_2700.png) ![5500 K: neutral studio white](img/comune/light_temperature_5500.png) ![9000 K: cool, bluish](img/comune/light_temperature_9000.png)

| Temperature | Light | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 2700 K | Household bulb | Cosy interiors, warm mood |
| 4000 K | Neutral-warm fluorescent | Offices, shops |
| 5500 K | Daylight, flash | Neutral product photos (true colors) |
| 6500 K | Overcast sky | Slightly cool, "technical" look |
| 9000 K | Shade, blue hour | Cold mood, backlights |

## Tips

- For **faithful colors** of the product keep all the lights at 5500 K.
- A **warm key light and a cool backlight** (the *Cinematic* preset) separate the product from
  the background.
- The temperature only changes the color, not the intensity: the brightest channel stays at
  full strength.
