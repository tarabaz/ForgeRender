# Light size and soft shadows

**Where:** select an **Area** light (**Selection** tab, or **Scene → Lights**) → **Width (m)** and **Height (m)**.

**What it does:** the size of the light seen from the object decides how soft the shadows are.
A small light gives sharp shadows and small, bright highlights; a large light wraps the object,
the shadows fade gradually (penumbra) and the highlights become wide and soft.

![Key light 0.15 m: sharp shadows, small highlights](img/comune/light_size_015.png) ![Key light 0.8 m: softer edges](img/comune/light_size_080.png) ![Key light 3 m: very soft shadows, wide highlights](img/comune/light_size_300.png)

*Same scene and same total power, only the size of the main softbox changes: 0.15 m, 0.8 m and
3 m (environment and fill kept low to make the shadows readable).*

## Intensity and size

The intensity of an area light is a **radiance**: the brightness of each square metre of its
surface. Enlarging the light at the same intensity therefore adds light. To keep the same
exposure when you enlarge it, lower the intensity in proportion to the area (twice the side =
one quarter of the intensity). The lighting presets already do this for you.

## Tips

- **Product photography:** large softboxes (as wide as the product or more) close to the
  object: soft shadows and clean, wide reflections on glossy surfaces.
- **Drama or texture:** a small or distant light brings out relief and texture with hard
  shadows.
- **Distance matters too:** the same softbox moved further away looks smaller and gives harder
  shadows.
